\title{The Spack Spec as a Mathematical Object\\
\large An order-theoretic semantics of \code{satisfies}, \code{intersects} and \code{constrain}}
\author{}
\date{2026-08-19 \\ \small branch \code{hs/test/property-based-tests}, tip \code{475f06ecef4}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A Spack spec is a constraint on build configurations, and three operations give
those constraints their algebra: \code{satisfies} compares two of them,
\code{intersects} tests whether they admit a common solution, and
\code{constrain} combines them.  This note defines the set of specs and the
three operations as mathematical objects.  The central claims: \code{satisfies}
is a \emph{preorder}, not a partial order, on a precisely delimited fragment of
the spec language; its kernel $\ker$ (mutual satisfaction) is an equivalence
whose quotient $\Spec/\!\ker$ is a partial order; \code{constrain} computes a
partial greatest lower bound, so the quotient is a meet-semilattice with a top
element and no bottom.  Each exclusion from the fragment is stated with its
counterexample: propagated variants (\code{++foo}) follow non-contradiction
instead of subset semantics and break transitivity; propagation policies on
edges (\code{\%\%}) and flags (\code{==}) are annotations invisible to the
order, which is what breaks antisymmetry; compiler flags are sequences whose
merge does not commute; and constructible specs denoting the empty set break
reflexivity of the denotational reading.  The order-theoretic core --- the
quotient construction, the descent of the partial meet, the collapse of
optimality annotations, product domains, and the simulation preorder on
dependency trees --- is machine-checked in Lean~4
(\code{SpecSemantics.lean}); the claims about the Python implementation are
validated by the property-based law checkers in
\code{lib/spack/spack/test/spec\_algebra*.py}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Spack's \code{Spec} is a single data structure with two readings.  Abstractly
it is a \emph{constraint}: \code{mpileaks @2.3: +debug \%gcc target=zen2:}
describes the set of builds the user would accept.  Concretely it is a
\emph{solution}: a fully resolved build with every version, variant, flag and
dependency pinned, identified by a DAG hash.  The bridge between the readings
is the relation
\[
  a \sat b \quad\iff\quad \code{a.satisfies(b)},
\]
read ``every build admitted by $a$ is admitted by $b$'', together with
\code{a.intersects(b)} (``some build is admitted by both'') and
\code{a.constrain(b)} (``narrow $a$ by $b$'', in place, raising when the two
are disjoint).  The in-tree law checkers state the intent
concisely:\src{lib/spack/spack/test/spec\_algebra.py:5--24}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  a.\code{satisfies}(b) \;&\approx\; \den{a} \subseteq \den{b},\\
  a.\code{intersects}(b) \;&\approx\; \den{a} \cap \den{b} \neq \emptyset,\\
  a.\code{constrain}(b) \;&\approx\; a \mathrel{:=} a \sqcap b,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\den{s}$ is the set of concrete specs $s$ can concretize to.

The first formalization attempt was ``the specs are a meet-semilattice under
$\sat$, with \code{constrain} as the meet''.  That claim is wrong twice over,
and both failures are informative.  First, $\sat$ is not antisymmetric:
\code{pkg-a \%gcc} and \code{pkg-a \%\%gcc} satisfy each other, yet they are
distinct states with distinct serializations, because \code{\%\%} adds a solver
preference that does not narrow the set of admissible solutions
(\S\ref{sec:solver}).  So at best the specs are a \emph{preorder}, and the
semilattice structure lives on the quotient by mutual satisfaction.  Second,
even the preorder claim needs a fragment restriction: propagated variants
(\code{++foo}) are deliberately compared by non-contradiction instead of by
subset, and that breaks transitivity outright (\S\ref{sec:propagation}).

This note makes the corrected claims precise.  It proceeds bottom-up: the
order-theoretic scaffolding (\S\ref{sec:order}), the per-dimension value
domains (\S\ref{sec:dimensions}), nodes and edges (\S\ref{sec:nodes},
\S\ref{sec:edges}), the assembled relation and its laws
(\S\ref{sec:algebra}), the distinction between the repo-free algebra and the
repo-aware readings (\S\ref{sec:repo}), and the solver's two-sorted
interpretation that explains the annotations (\S\ref{sec:solver}).
\S\ref{sec:audit} then turns the framework on the implementation: an
adversarial audit, with executed witnesses, of where the code contradicts
the mathematics it approximates.
\S\ref{sec:lean} maps every general theorem to its machine-checked Lean
counterpart and every implementation claim to the property test that guards
it, and discusses how far such a bridge between proof and implementation can
be pushed.

\begin{convention}
$\Spec$ is the set of \code{Spec} states (Python objects up to
\code{to\_dict()} equality); $\Conc \subseteq \Spec$ the concrete ones.
$a \sat b$ abbreviates \code{a.satisfies(b)}.  $a \meet b$ denotes
\code{spack.spec.meet(a,b)}: the value of \code{constrain} run on a copy of
$a$, with $a \meet b = \bot$ (undefined) when \code{constrain} raises.  File
and line references are to the branch tip above.
\end{convention}

\section{Order-theoretic scaffolding}\label{sec:order}

Everything in this section is general order theory, independent of Spack, and
is machine-checked in \code{SpecSemantics.lean}; theorem names below refer to
that file.

\begin{definition}[Preorder, kernel]
A \emph{preorder} on a set $X$ is a reflexive, transitive relation
$\sqsubseteq$.  Its \emph{kernel} is $x \ker y \iff x \sqsubseteq y \wedge y
\sqsubseteq x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Antisymmetrization; \code{Pre.setoid}, \code{Pre.qle\_antisymm}]
\label{thm:quotient}
The kernel of a preorder is an equivalence relation, $\sqsubseteq$ descends to
the quotient $X/\!\ker$, and the descended relation is a partial order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Partial meet]\label{def:meet}
A \emph{partial meet} for a preorder $(X, \sqsubseteq)$ is a partial binary
operation $\meet$ with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(M\arabic*),itemsep=1pt]
  \item \emph{sound}: if $x \meet y$ is defined then $x \meet y \sqsubseteq x$
        and $x \meet y \sqsubseteq y$;
  \item \emph{greatest}: if $x \meet y$ is defined and $c \sqsubseteq x$,
        $c \sqsubseteq y$, then $c \sqsubseteq x \meet y$;
  \item \emph{total on compatible pairs}: if some $c$ has $c \sqsubseteq x$ and
        $c \sqsubseteq y$, then $x \meet y$ is defined.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

(M1)--(M3) are exactly the contract the law checkers impose on
\code{constrain}:\src{lib/spack/spack/test/spec\_algebra\_corpus.py:225--256,
337--352}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item (M1) is \code{constrained\_lhs\_satisfies\_rhs} together with\newline
      \code{constrain\_never\_weakens\_the\_lhs};
\item (M2) is \code{constrain\_is\_the\_greatest\_lower\_bound};
\item (M3) is one direction of \code{intersects\_agrees\_with\_constrain}:
      \code{constrain} raises exactly when \code{intersects} is false, and
      \code{intersects} may only be false when no common refinement exists.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Meet laws up to kernel; \code{Meet.idem/comm/assoc/congr}]
\label{thm:meetlaws}
A partial meet is idempotent, commutative and associative up to $\ker$
(associativity including definedness: if $(x \meet y) \meet z$ is defined then
so is $x \meet (y \meet z)$, with $\ker$-equivalent results), and it is a
$\ker$-congruence: $x \ker x'$, $y \ker y'$ give equal definedness and
$\ker$-equivalent results.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Descent; \code{Meet.qmeet\_idem/comm/assoc}]
\label{thm:descent}
On $X/\!\ker$ the partial meet induces a well-defined partial operation
satisfying idempotence, commutativity and associativity as \emph{equalities}.
Thus $X/\!\ker$ is a meet-semilattice, partial only on genuinely incompatible
pairs.
\end{theorem}

The next theorem is the abstract form of the \code{\%}/\code{\%\%} dispute.

\begin{theorem}[Annotation collapse; \code{Pre.ann}, \code{Pre.annIso}]
\label{thm:annotation}
Let $(X, \sqsubseteq)$ be a preorder and $\Pi$ a nonempty set of annotations.
Order $X \times \Pi$ by first components only: $(x,p) \sqsubseteq (y,q) \iff
x \sqsubseteq y$.  Then $(x,p) \ker (y,q) \iff x \ker y$, and the quotient
$(X \times \Pi)/\!\ker$ is order-isomorphic to $X/\!\ker$.
\end{theorem}

In words: a dimension that \code{satisfies} does not read is erased by the
quotient, no matter how many states it distinguishes.  Antisymmetry of the
\emph{state} order is unrecoverable while such a dimension exists; the partial
order lives on the quotient and is indifferent to the annotation.

\begin{theorem}[Products; \code{Pre.prod}, \code{Meet.prod}]\label{thm:prod}
The product of preorders, ordered pointwise, is a preorder; pointwise partial
meets satisfying (M1)--(M3) give a partial meet on the product, defined
exactly when every component is defined.
\end{theorem}

\section{The value domains}\label{sec:dimensions}

A spec node constrains several independent \emph{dimensions}.  Each dimension
is a preorder with a top element $\top$ (``unconstrained'') and a partial meet;
by Theorem~\ref{thm:prod} the node inherits both.  This section defines each
dimension's carrier, order, meet and canonical form.

\subsection{Names and namespaces}\label{sec:names}

\begin{definition}[Flat domain]
For a set $A$, the \emph{flat domain} $A_\top = A \cup \{\top\}$ is ordered by
$x \sqsubseteq y \iff y = \top \vee x = y$.  The meet keeps the non-$\top$
side and is undefined on two distinct elements of $A$.
\end{definition}

The name dimension is $\Names_\top$ where $\Names$ is the set of package
names; the implementation writes $\top$ as the empty string.\src{spec.py:1931;
the parser also maps \code{*} to it, spec\_parser.py:459--462}  The namespace
dimension is the same construction with \code{None} as
$\top$.\src{spec.py:1938, 3906--3907}  Two distinct names are disjoint
\emph{by fiat}, even when one names a virtual the other provides: resolving
providers would make the operations depend on the configured repositories
(\S\ref{sec:repo}).\src{spec.py:3529--3533}  In the flat domain the kernel is
trivial ($x \ker y \iff x = y$), so this dimension contributes no quotient
collapse.  (Lean: \code{flat}, \code{flatMeet}.)

\subsection{Versions}\label{sec:versions}

The version dimension has the richest canonical form.

\begin{definition}[Version points]
A \emph{standard version} is a pair (component tuple, prerelease tuple), where
components are integers or ordered string atoms (with the ``infinity''
names \code{develop}, \code{main}, \code{master}, \dots\ above every numeric
component) and prereleases range over
$\mathit{alpha} < \mathit{beta} < \mathit{rc} < \mathit{final}$; the order is
lexicographic and total.\src{version/version\_types.py:110--123, 221--515}
A \emph{git version} is a distinct point identified by a commit or ref; a git
version and a standard version never satisfy one
another.\src{version\_types.py:375--376, 633--634}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Version constraints]
A \emph{range} $\code{lo:hi}$ denotes the half-open interval
$[\mathit{lo}, \mathrm{next}(\mathit{hi}))$ over standard versions, where
$\mathrm{next}$ is the successor in the lexicographic
order.\src{version\_types.py:792--937}  A \code{VersionList} is a sorted list
of points and ranges in which overlapping or adjacent elements are merged on
insertion, so the representation of each denotable set is
\emph{unique}.\src{version\_types.py:972--1009}  The order is set inclusion of
denotations, the meet is set intersection, and
$\top = \code{@:}$.\src{version\_types.py:1052--1132}
\end{definition}

Surface syntax: \code{@1.2} denotes the range $[1.2, \mathrm{next}(1.2))$ ---
so \code{@1.2.1} satisfies it --- while \code{@=1.2} denotes the single point
$1.2$, which \code{@1.2.1} does not satisfy (verified).  Because the
representation is canonical, this dimension satisfies antisymmetry as
\emph{state} equality: the kernel is trivial and the meet laws hold on the
nose.  This is the model the target dimension was brought to by the
canonicalization work in PR~52856 (\S\ref{sec:arch}).

\subsection{Variants}\label{sec:variants}

\begin{definition}[Variant value]\label{def:variant}
Let $\Sigma$ be the set of value strings.  A variant value is a tuple
$(\tau, \pi, c, W)$ with type $\tau \in \{\textsc{bool} < \textsc{single} <
\textsc{multi}\}$, propagation bit $\pi \in \Bool$, concreteness bit
$c \in \Bool$, and a finite value set $W \subseteq \Sigma \cup \Bool$,
subject to: $\tau \in \{\textsc{bool}, \textsc{single}\}$ forces $c = 1$ and
$|W| = 1$.\src{variant.py:274--317; values are deduplicated and sorted, so
$W$ is genuinely a set, variant.py:387--404}  The wildcard \code{foo=*} is
$(\textsc{multi}, \pi, 0, \emptyset)$.\src{variant.py:348--349}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Variant order and meet, $\pi$ ignored]\label{def:variantorder}
Write $x = (\tau_x, \pi_x, c_x, W_x)$, similarly $y$.  Then\src{variant.py:440--487}
\[
  x \sat y \iff
  \begin{cases}
    W_y \subseteq W_x & \text{if } c_y = 0 \quad\text{(``at least these values'')},\\
    W_x = W_y         & \text{if } c_y = 1 \wedge c_x = 1,\\
    \text{false}      & \text{if } c_y = 1 \wedge c_x = 0,
  \end{cases}
\]
with one refinement: for the reserved \code{patches} variant, membership is
tested by checksum-prefix instead of equality.  The meet is
$(\min(\tau_x,\tau_y),\, \pi_x \wedge \pi_y,\, c_x \vee c_y,\, W_x \cup W_y)$,
undefined iff the two sides are concrete with different value sets, or one is
concrete and misses a value of the other.
\end{definition}

Denotationally: an abstract multi-value denotes the up-set
$\{V : W \subseteq V\}$ of value assignments, a concrete value denotes exactly
$\{W\}$, and \code{foo=*} denotes every assignment that defines \code{foo} at
all --- it is the top element \emph{among specs that mention the variant};
omitting the variant entirely is the true top for the dimension.
A \code{VariantMap} is a finite partial map from variant names to variant
values; a missing key is $\top$, and the map order and meet are pointwise
(Theorem~\ref{thm:prod} again, indexed over names).

Note what Definition~\ref{def:variantorder} does \emph{not} read: the
propagation bit $\pi$.  Its semantics is deferred to \S\ref{sec:propagation};
it is the variant-level instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:annotation} in the
node-local order, but --- unlike the edge policy --- it is \emph{intended} to
constrain the solution set, which is exactly why it is a gap and not an
annotation (\S\ref{sec:propagation}).

In the multi-valued abstract case the kernel is trivial because $W$ is stored
as a set.  Had values been stored as a list, the quotient would identify
reorderings and duplicates --- the Lean development includes this model
(\code{multi}, \code{multiMeet}: lists ordered by reverse membership, meet =
concatenation, with $[1,2] \ker [2,1,1]$ proved) both as the general shape of
set-like dimensions and as a worked example of a nontrivial kernel.

\subsection{Compiler flags}\label{sec:flags}

The flag dimension is the one place where the meet is not commutative even up
to the kernel's collapse of state.

\begin{definition}[Flag map]
A flag map assigns to each flag type (\code{cflags}, \code{cxxflags},
\code{fflags}, \code{ldflags}, \code{ldlibs}, \code{cppflags}) a finite
\emph{sequence} of flags, each a string with a propagation bit
$\pi$.\src{spec.py:1088--1139}  The order compares \emph{sets}: $F \sat G$
iff for every type $t$, the value set of $F(t)$ contains that of $G(t)$,
ignoring order and $\pi$ in both directions.\src{spec.py:1142--1143}  The meet
appends the missing flags of the right operand to the left operand's sequence
and promotes $\pi$ on value matches; it never fails.\src{spec.py:1145--1175}
\end{definition}

Order matters to the build (\code{-O2 -g} and \code{-g -O2} are different
command lines), so the sequence is the honest state; but the order is
invisible to $\sat$, so the kernel identifies permutations:
$\den{\code{cflags='-O2 -g'}} = \den{\code{cflags='-g -O2'}}$ under the
declared reading of $\sat$, while the two are distinct states.  The meet
$\code{cflags=-O2} \meet \code{cflags=-g}$ produces \code{cflags='-O2 -g'} one
way and \code{cflags='-g -O2'} the other (verified): commutativity holds only
up to $\ker$, and the state-level law is pinned as failing by
design.\src{spec\_algebra.py:300--306}  The property harness additionally
excuses quotient-level failures of associativity, monotonicity and congruence
on operand pairs carrying distinct flags of one type\src{spec\_algebra\_%
properties.py:725--736} --- the flag dimension is where the ``sequence with
set semantics'' compromise leaks into the algebra, and a fully lawful
treatment (e.g.\ a free-monoid quotient with an order-aware $\sat$) is an
open design question (\S\ref{sec:open}).

\subsection{Architecture}\label{sec:arch}

The architecture dimension is a triple (platform, operating system, target).
Platform and OS are flat domains enriched with a wildcard: the carrier is
$\{\top\} \cup \{*\} \cup A$, where $*$ satisfies exactly ``set to
something'': $x \sat * \iff x \neq \top$, and $*$ intersects
everything.\src{spec.py:355--369}  The meet resolves $*$ against a name by
taking the name.\src{spec.py:701--717}

Targets are richer because microarchitectures form a DAG, not a chain.

\begin{definition}[Target domain]
Let $(U, \le)$ be the finite poset of microarchitectures, $u \le v$ iff $v$ is
$u$ or an ancestor of $u$ (so \code{zen2} $\le$ \code{x86\_64}); each $u$ has
a unique maximal ancestor, its \emph{family}.\src{vendor/archspec:
microarchitecture.py:110--205}  A \emph{target expression} is a finite union
of intervals, each written \code{lo:hi} and denoting
$\{u : \mathit{lo} \le u \le \mathit{hi}\}$, with a missing bound resolving to
the family root (below) or to no constraint (above).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical target form]\label{def:targetcanon}
Every target expression is stored canonically\src{spec.py:219--247
(\code{\_canonical\_target\_range})}: per interval, a lower bound equal to the
family of the upper bound is dropped, \code{lo:hi} with $lo = hi$ collapses to
the point, and an upper bound that is itself a family root collapses to the
bare root (\code{:aarch64}, \code{aarch64:aarch64} and \code{aarch64} are one
state); across intervals, duplicates and intervals contained in another are
dropped and the rest sorted.  The canonical form is a sorted,
subsumption-free antichain of intervals.
\end{definition}

The order is containment of denoted sets and the meet is intersection,
computed per interval pair; because $(U, \le)$ is not a lattice, a pair of
bounds has a \emph{set} of minimal upper bounds and maximal lower bounds, so
one intersection can produce several intervals --- e.g.\
$\code{cascadelake:} \meet \code{cannonlake:} = \code{icelake:}$ and
$\code{armv8.6a:} \meet \code{neoverse\_n1:} =
\code{ampere1:,ampere1a:}$ (verified).\src{spec.py:301--346, 407--439}
Canonicalization makes the kernel trivial on this dimension:
\code{:icelake} and \code{x86\_64:icelake} are one state with one DAG hash
(verified).  One known incompleteness remains: the implemented containment
test covers each left interval by a \emph{single} right interval, so a left
interval covered only by the union of two right intervals is reported
unsatisfied.\src{spec.py:386--390}  This makes $\sat$ on targets sound but not
complete for the denotational reading.

\subsection{Abstract hash}

The hash dimension's carrier is $\{\top\} \cup B^{*}$, prefixes over the
base-32 alphabet, written \code{/abc123}.  The order is
``\emph{my} hash extends \emph{your} prefix'': $h \sat p$ iff $p$ is a prefix
of $h$ (with a concrete spec contributing its full DAG hash); two prefixes are
compatible iff one extends the other, and the meet keeps the
longer.\src{spec.py:3549--3555, 3689--3693, 3899--3904}  Denotationally a
prefix denotes the set of concrete specs whose DAG hash extends it.

\section{Nodes}\label{sec:nodes}

\begin{definition}[Abstract node]\label{def:node}
An \emph{abstract node} is an element of the product
\[
  N \;=\;
  \underbrace{\Names_\top}_{\text{name}} \times
  \underbrace{\mathit{Ns}_\top}_{\text{namespace}} \times
  \underbrace{\mathit{Ver}}_{\text{versions}} \times
  \underbrace{\mathit{Var}}_{\text{variant map}} \times
  \underbrace{\mathit{Flag}}_{\text{flag map}} \times
  \underbrace{\mathit{Arch}_\top}_{\text{architecture}} \times
  \underbrace{\mathit{Hash}_\top}_{\text{abstract hash}},
\]
ordered and met pointwise (Theorem~\ref{thm:prod}); the top element (every
coordinate $\top$) is the \emph{anonymous} node.\src{node equality is
\code{\_cmp\_node}, spec.py:4272--4283; the anonymous spec, spec.py:2445--2447}
A \emph{concrete node} additionally carries a package hash (content hash of
the build recipe) and a frozen map of \emph{provided virtuals}
$\mathit{pv} : \Names \rightharpoonup \mathit{Ver}$
(\S\ref{sec:repo}).\src{spec.py:1954--1956, 3339--3355}
\end{definition}

Concreteness is a mode, not a derived property: the concretizer asserts it,
and with it the invariants that every variant value is concrete, the version
is a single point, and the DAG hash is defined.\src{spec.py:3263--3315}  Node
comparison short-circuits against a concrete right operand: $a \sat c$ iff
$a$ is concrete and $\mathrm{hash}(a) = \mathrm{hash}(c)$ --- a concrete spec
is a singleton, and the order restricted to $\Conc$ is
discrete.\src{spec.py:3874--3880}

\begin{definition}[Denotation]\label{def:denotation}
$\den{s} = \{\, c \in \Conc : c \sat s \,\}$.
\end{definition}

With Definition~\ref{def:denotation} the slogan ``$\sat$ is $\subseteq$''
becomes a theorem schema to be checked, not assumed: $a \sat b \Rightarrow
\den{a} \subseteq \den{b}$ holds by transitivity wherever $\sat$ is
transitive, while the converse (completeness) genuinely fails --- two specs
can denote the same set without satisfying each other, e.g.\ through the
target incompleteness above, and a spec can denote $\emptyset$ without $\sat$
detecting it (\S\ref{sec:empty}).  The property harness states this
restraint explicitly: over its finite universe of maximal specs it asserts
only the forward direction.\src{spec\_algebra\_properties.py:1001--1011}

One asymmetry deserves a note: state equality of abstract specs
(\code{\_cmp\_node}) reads exactly the seven coordinates of
Definition~\ref{def:node}, while the DAG hash of concrete specs also covers
externals, extra attributes, splice provenance and the frozen provided
virtuals.  The formal reading: abstract state is the seven-tuple; concrete
state is the full serialization, compared via the hash.

\section{Edges and the dependency structure}\label{sec:edges}

\begin{definition}[Edge]\label{def:edge}
An edge from a parent node to a child spec is a tuple
\[
  e = (\,d,\; V,\; \delta,\; w,\; \rho\,),
\]
where $d \subseteq \{\textsc{build}, \textsc{link}, \textsc{run},
\textsc{test}\}$ is the set of dependency types, read as a lower bound;
$V \subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}} \Names$ the virtuals the parent takes from the
child; $\delta \in \Bool$ distinguishes direct (\code{\%}) from transitive
(\code{\^{}}); $w \in \Spec$ is the condition (\code{when=}), with $\top$ for
unconditional; and $\rho \in \{\textsc{none}, \textsc{preference}\}$ is the
propagation policy (\code{\%\%}).
$\rho = \textsc{preference}$ requires $\delta = 1$.\src{spec.py:864--911;
enums.py:30--34}  A \emph{spec} is a rooted acyclic multigraph of nodes and
edges; parallel edges to one name are permitted and meaningful (two
\code{\^{}pkg-b} clauses may be witnessed by two distinct nodes).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Edge order]\label{def:edgeorder}
Edge $e_1$ (on the candidate side) satisfies edge $e_2$ (on the constraint
side) iff the child names match or $e_2$'s child name is in $V_1$; $d_2
\subseteq d_1$; $V_2 \subseteq V_1$; the conditions compare contravariantly
($w_2 \sat w_1$: the constraint's condition must apply in no more cases than
the candidate's, so an unconditional candidate edge satisfies a conditional
constraint and not conversely); the
child specs compare recursively; and if $e_2$ narrows a virtual's versions,
the candidate child must provide that virtual at those versions (frozen
virtuals, \S\ref{sec:repo}).  The policy $\rho$ is \emph{not
read}.\src{spec.py:1829--1858}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Simulation]\label{def:simulation}
$a \sat b$ on the graph level: the root nodes compare per
Definition~\ref{def:node}, and for every edge constraint of $b$ there is a
witnessing edge of $a$ --- for a direct constraint ($\delta = 1$) an edge at
depth one that is direct (unless $a$ is concrete, where the direct flag is
erased), for a transitive constraint an edge whose child lies in the
appropriate closure of $a$ (link/run closure plus direct edges) --- whose
attributes and child satisfy the constraint per
Definition~\ref{def:edgeorder}.  Distinct constraint edges may be witnessed by
distinct parallel edges; the search backtracks across same-name
candidates.\src{spec.py:1719--1791; exhaustiveness per PR~52876}
\end{definition}

This is a simulation preorder in the process-calculus sense.  Its two
structural properties are exactly the ones the bug history shows are easy to
lose, and both are proved for the tree model in Lean
(\code{Tree.TLe.refl}, \code{Tree.TLe.trans}): reflexivity requires every edge
to be able to witness itself, and transitivity requires witnesses to compose.
The historical failures are instructive: an anonymous spec satisfying every
named one made $\sat$ non-transitive (\code{pkg-a} $\sat$ \code{""} $\sat$
\code{pkg-b}, fixed in PR~52861), and the depth-one shortcut let
\code{\^{}pkg-b} witness \code{\%pkg-b} (fixed in PR~52860).

\paragraph{Conditional edges.}
An edge's condition $w$ is evaluated against the finished node by parser and
merge alike, to a fixpoint: a condition the node falsifies removes the edge, a
condition the node forces makes the edge unconditional, and a condition that
constrains dependencies stays undecided (verified: \code{pkg-a\char`~foo
\^{}[when='+foo'] pkg-b@1} parses to \code{pkg-a\char`~foo}, and
\code{pkg-a+foo \^{}[when='+foo'] pkg-b@1} to \code{pkg-a+foo
\^{}pkg-b@1}).\src{spec.py:3775--3806}  This canonicalization is what keeps
edge-level absorption lawful; the residual non-minimality (several conditional
edges jointly equivalent to one broader edge are not fused, because
implication is checked pairwise) is a documented simplicity trade-off that
affects only which representative of a $\ker$-class the merge
returns.\src{spec\_algebra\_properties.py:639--657}

\paragraph{The meet on graphs.}
\code{constrain} merges roots per Definition~\ref{def:node} and then unions
the edge lists, with three refinements: an edge implied by another is dropped
(absorption, including a named edge absorbing an anonymous requirement); a
direct edge meets its \code{\_same\_direct\_dep} partner (same name, same
condition) by merging attributes pointwise; and a child named after a virtual
in the partner's $V$ is renamed to the provider.  The meet on graphs has
\emph{no} canonical minimal form --- a virtual edge and its provider edge stay
side by side, parallel deptype edges stay parallel --- so on the graph level
the kernel is genuinely nontrivial and the semilattice structure lives on the
quotient.\src{spec.py:2299--2418; spec\_algebra.py:309--350}

\section{The algebra of satisfies and constrain}\label{sec:algebra}

We can now state the main claims at the level the law checkers verify them.

\begin{definition}[The lawful fragment]\label{def:fragment}
Let $\Spec_0 \subseteq \Spec$ be the specs containing no propagated variant
(\code{++foo}, \S\ref{sec:propagation}), no bare git ref version (whose
comparison consults a repository), and no node denoting $\emptyset$ through a
jointly-forced group of direct provider edges (\S\ref{sec:empty}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Preorder]\label{thm:preorder}
On $\Spec_0$, $\sat$ is reflexive and transitive.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Status.}  Reflexivity and transitivity are checked exhaustively over a
79-spec corpus (quadratic and cubic sweeps) and by randomized property
sweeps across nineteen syntactic dimensions with shrinking; the branch tip is
clean.\src{spec\_algebra\_corpus.py; spec\_algebra\_properties.py}  For the
tree model of Definition~\ref{def:simulation} both properties are proved
(Lean, \code{Tree.pre}).  The claim for the full implementation is empirical,
not proved: the checked fragment is the generator's language, and the
excluded features each break a law provably (below).

\begin{theorem}[Kernel and quotient]\label{thm:kernel}
On $\Spec_0$, $\ker$ (mutual satisfaction) is an equivalence;
$\Spec_0/\!\ker$ is a partial order with top element the class of the
anonymous spec.  The kernel is nontrivial: distinct states satisfy each other.
\end{theorem}

The interesting content is the catalog of nontrivial kernel classes:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Propagation policies on edges}: \code{pkg-a \%pkg-b} $\ker$
      \code{pkg-a \%\%pkg-b}, distinct states, distinct serializations,
      distinct \code{==}/hash (verified; the policy entered state equality in
      PR~52905).  This is Theorem~\ref{thm:annotation} with $\Pi$ the policy
      set; \S\ref{sec:solver} explains why the annotation must survive the
      meet even though the order ignores it.
\item \emph{Propagation bits on flags}: \code{cflags==-O2} $\ker$
      \code{cflags=-O2} (verified), same construction.
\item \emph{Flag sequence order}: \code{cflags='-O2 -g'} $\ker$
      \code{cflags='-g -O2'} (\S\ref{sec:flags}).
\item \emph{Unfused edge presentations}: a spec with edges
      \code{\^{}mpi@3} and \code{\^{}[virtuals=mpi] mpich} beside each other
      is $\ker$-equivalent to presentations a smarter merge would fuse; the
      merge deliberately does not fuse them.\src{spec\_algebra.py:313--350}
\item \emph{Jointly-split conditions}: \code{\%[when='+v'] x} beside
      \code{\%[when='\char`~v'] x} versus the unconditional edge.
\end{enumerate}

Classes 1--2 are \emph{permanent}: they encode optimality data the order must
not read (\S\ref{sec:solver}).  Classes 3--5 are representation slack: a
stronger normal form could shrink them, at implementation cost, without
changing the quotient.

\begin{theorem}[Meet]\label{thm:constrainmeet}
On $\Spec_0$ minus the flag-order slack (operand pairs carrying distinct
flags of one type), $\meet$ satisfies (M1)--(M3) of
Definition~\ref{def:meet}.  Hence, by Theorems~\ref{thm:meetlaws}
and~\ref{thm:descent}, $\Spec_0/\!\ker$ is a meet-semilattice: idempotent,
commutative, associative, monotone
($a \sat b \Rightarrow a \meet c \sat b \meet c$ where defined), with
absorption ($a \sat b \Rightarrow a \meet b \ker a$), partial exactly on
pairs with no common refinement, and agreeing with \code{intersects}
($a \meet b$ defined $\iff$ \code{a.intersects(b)}).
\end{theorem}

\emph{Status.}  Every named law is a checked law in the harness, including the
exact greatest-lower-bound biconditional over a finite universe of 864
maximal specs ($t \sat a \meet b \iff t \sat a \wedge t \sat b$, both
directions --- the forward direction catches a meet that admits too much, the
backward one a meet that lost a solution).\src{spec\_algebra\_properties.py:%
1106--1130}  The descent from (M1)--(M3) to the semilattice is proved once and
for all (Lean); what remains empirical is that \code{constrain} satisfies
(M1)--(M3).

\emph{No bottom.}  No spec state denotes $\emptyset$ lawfully, so the
semilattice has no bottom element; \code{constrain} raises instead of
producing one, and \code{meet} adjoins an artificial $\bot$ as
\code{None}.\src{spec.py:5630--5636}

Beyond the order laws, the harness pins \emph{representation} laws that a
purely order-theoretic account would miss but that the algebra relies on:
\code{constrain} is atomic (a failed merge leaves the left operand
untouched), never mutates the right operand, reports its change flag
honestly, leaves the narrowing dimensions of an already-satisfying left
operand untouched (state-level absorption), is invariant under edge storage
order, and keeps edge maps keyed by the child's current
name.\src{spec\_algebra\_properties.py:860--1057; spec\_algebra\_corpus.py:%
183--320}

\subsection{Variant propagation: the deliberate breach}\label{sec:propagation}

The syntax \code{++foo} (and \code{foo==bar}, \code{\char`~\char`~foo})
asserts a variant value \emph{and} requests its propagation through the DAG.
The intended semantics, and the one the concretizer implements, is a guarded
implication: \emph{every node (root included) that defines the variant takes
the value}; formally, for each node $n$ of the solution,
$\code{foo} \in \mathrm{dom}(n) \Rightarrow n.\code{foo} = v$ --- equivalently
``\code{foo=*} implies \code{+foo}'' per node.\src{solver/concretize.lp:%
1715--1719: \code{variant\_value} is derived only under
\code{node\_has\_variant} and \code{variant\_possible\_value}; asp.py:%
2282--2286 emits even the root's own value conditionally}

The \code{Spec} object stores something weaker: the same node-local value as
\code{+foo} plus one bit, with no guard.\src{spec.py:2206--2208;
variant.py:285}  Because the node-local reading (unconditional) and the
solver's reading (guarded, DAG-wide) differ, \code{satisfies} implements
neither as a subset test; it falls back to \emph{non-contradiction}: no node
of the candidate may contradict the propagated value, and a spec with no
opinion passes.\src{spec.py:3945--3982}

\begin{counterexample}[Transitivity fails on \code{++}]\label{cex:prop}
All verified:
\[
\code{pkg-a\char`~foo} \sat \code{pkg-a}, \qquad
\code{pkg-a} \sat \code{pkg-a++foo}, \qquad
\code{pkg-a\char`~foo} \not\sat \code{pkg-a++foo}.
\]
Moreover $\sat$ is not sound for the denotational reading here:
$\den{\code{pkg-a}} \not\subseteq \den{\code{pkg-a++foo}}$, yet
$\code{pkg-a} \sat \code{pkg-a++foo}$.  And the meet demotes the bit ---
$\code{pkg-a++foo} \meet \code{pkg-a+foo} = \code{pkg-a+foo}$ (verified) ---
so on this fragment $\meet$ is not even a lower bound.  A concretization-level
witness that the stored state over-asserts: \code{callpath++debug}
concretizes with \code{\^{}mpich+debug} but \emph{without} \code{+debug} on
the root (the mock \code{callpath} defines no \code{debug} variant), while
the abstract spec object reports \code{debug} locally
true.\src{test/concretization/core.py:854--862}
\end{counterexample}

This is why $\Spec_0$ excludes \code{++}: the corpus filters propagated
variants out of the ordered checks, and the sweep excuses their failures via
an explicit gap predicate that names this
subsection's reason.\src{spec\_algebra\_corpus.py:149--160;
spec\_algebra\_properties.py:581--588}  The repair path is equally explicit:
store the guarded constraint separately from the node-local value --- i.e.\
represent \code{++foo} as the pair (propagation request, \emph{conditional}
local assertion guarded by ``\code{foo} is defined here'') instead of
(unconditional local assertion, bit).  With the guard in the state, $\sat$
can evaluate it per node and the subset semantics returns; the three
conventions in \S\ref{sec:solver} then reduce to two.

\subsection{The empty denotation}\label{sec:empty}

A node with two direct provider edges of one virtual whose conditions the
node jointly forces denotes $\emptyset$: each solution takes the virtual from
exactly one direct dependency.  The parser and the merge reject the states
they can see (verified: \code{+foo \%[when='+foo' virtuals=c] gcc
\%[virtuals=c] llvm} raises at parse; the meet of \code{+foo} with the
undecided spec is $\bot$ and \code{intersects} reports false both ways), but
the state remains constructible through paths that compare conditions only
pairwise.  On such a spec, honest \code{intersects} reports
$a \cap a = \emptyset$ --- and reflexivity of the \emph{denotational} reading
($\emptyset \subseteq \emptyset$) collides with the implemented $\sat$, which
is why $\Spec_0$ excludes these states.\src{spec\_algebra\_properties.py:%
676--701; spec\_algebra.py:544--574}  The clean resolution would be a
recognized bottom element: either make construction reject every empty state
(closing the pairwise-comparison loophole) or admit $\bot$ into the algebra
with $\bot \sat x$ for all $x$.  Bug no.~51 of the ledger (a forced
condition with conflicting deps intersects everything) is this section's
intersects-side twin.

\section{Free constraints and the repository}\label{sec:repo}

Everything above is \emph{repo-free}: on this branch, \code{satisfies},
\code{intersects} and \code{constrain} are functions of their two operands.
No call in their call graph consults the configured package repositories
(the one residual exception: comparing a bare git ref version looks up the
package's git history, which is why $\Spec_0$ excludes bare refs).  Names are
opaque tokens --- \code{Spec("mpich")} does not satisfy \code{Spec("mpi")},
and the two do not intersect (verified) --- and unknown names behave like any
other token.  This was a deliberate semantic decision (``stateless
comparison''), replacing an older regime where the verdicts of the three
operations changed with the active repository set.

\begin{definition}[Repository]\label{def:repo}
A repository is a partial map $R : \Names \rightharpoonup \mathit{Pkg}$,
where a package signature $\mathit{Pkg}$ consists of when-indexed families
(each key a \emph{condition spec}, evaluated by the repo-free $\sat$):
declared versions; variant definitions (name $\mapsto$ type, admissible
values, default, multiplicity); conditional dependencies (constraint spec,
dependency types, patches); provided virtuals (virtual specs with version
ranges); conflicts and requirements.\src{package\_base.py:549--577}
\end{definition}

The repository induces a second, finer denotation: $\denR{s} \subseteq
\den{s}$, the concrete specs that are also \emph{valid instances} of $R$ ---
every node's name in $\mathrm{dom}(R)$, version among the declared ones,
variant values admissible under the definitions whose conditions hold, edges
generated by the dependency directives, virtuals wired to actual providers.
For a fixed repository the admissible node states form a \emph{finite} set
(finitely many names, declared versions, variant assignments, targets), so
$R$-valid concrete specs are finite graphs over a finite node alphabet ---
the ``sparse graph on a finite set of possible nodes'' reading of
concretization, with the ASP solver selecting among them.

The interplay between the free algebra and $R$ is a projection performed at
well-defined moments, after which the algebra is free again:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Construction-time projection.}  \code{substitute\_abstract\_%
variants} maps the free variant constraint (\textsc{multi}, abstract) onto
the package's declared definition, assigning the type and concreteness of
Definition~\ref{def:variant} and rejecting undeclared names or inadmissible
values; \code{validate\_or\_raise} checks names, virtuals and
dependencies.\src{spec.py:5639--5691, 3389--3413}  These are partial
functions $\pi_R : \Spec \rightharpoonup \Spec$ raising outside their
domain; they refine states and never relax them.
\item \emph{Concretization-time freezing.}  Immediately before a spec is
marked concrete, the versions of every virtual it provides are computed from
$R$ (as the \emph{intersection} of the ranges of all \code{provides} clauses
whose conditions hold) and stored on the node; the frozen map enters the DAG
hash.\src{repo.py:2116--2142; spec.py:3350--3356}  After freezing, virtual
queries against concrete specs --- ``does this \code{mpich} node satisfy
\code{\^{}mpi@3}?'' --- are decided from node state alone.  The design
pattern: instead of parameterizing the order by $R$, the $R$-facts the order
needs are baked into the state, once, at a moment with a well-defined
repository.
\item \emph{Explicitly stateful refinement.}  The surviving repo-aware
comparison is the method \code{PackageBase.intersects}.  It starts from the free
\code{intersects} and may only strengthen \code{True} to \code{False}
(e.g.\ via single-valuedness of a declared variant).  Its docstring states
the division of labor: by design, \code{Spec.intersects()} knows nothing
about package metadata.\src{package\_base.py:1472--1529}
\end{enumerate}

In order-theoretic terms: the free specs are the constraint language
generated by the grammar, with $\sat$ and $\meet$ defined structurally ---
``free'' in the informal sense that no relations beyond the structural ones
hold, so it is the coarsest lawful algebra over the syntax.  A repository
does not change the order; it selects the subset of concrete points
($\denR{\cdot}$), refines abstract states through the partial projection
$\pi_R$, and supplies the facts that freezing internalizes.  Whether
$\pi_R$ enjoys an adjoint-like universal property (the least $R$-valid
refinement of a free spec) is open; \code{substitute\_abstract\_variants}
behaves like one on the variant dimension.

\section{The solver's reading: constraints and preferences}\label{sec:solver}

The disputes that forced the preorder correction dissolve once the denotation
is two-sorted.  A spec contributes to the solver both \emph{hard} clauses,
which cut the solution space, and \emph{soft} data, which steers the
optimization among admissible solutions.  Write
\[
  \llbracket s \rrbracket \;=\; (\den{s},\; \mathit{pref}_s),
\]
where $\mathit{pref}_s$ collects the preference contributions.  The relation
$\sat$ compares only the first component --- this is the design decision,
made explicit in the code comment that a propagated edge ``is an input to the
solver's objective even where it does not narrow the solution
set''.\src{spec.py:1884--1886}  Theorem~\ref{thm:annotation} then gives the
structure: the state is $(\text{constraint}, \text{annotation})$, the order
reads the constraint, the kernel erases the annotation, and the quotient is
the constraint order.  Two consequences follow.  Antisymmetry of states is
gone for good --- restoring it would mean either reading preferences in
$\sat$ (wrong: \code{\%\%gcc} admits every solution \code{\%gcc} does) or
dropping them from the state (wrong: the merge would lose solver input,
which was bug no.~36).  And the meet must preserve the annotation even
though the order ignores it; the implementation promotes
(\textsc{none} $\meet$ \textsc{preference} = \textsc{preference}) on edges
and flags.

The three propagation mechanisms occupy three different points of this
two-sorted space:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
 & stored as & solver reading & $\sat$ reading \\
\midrule
\code{\%\%dep} & edge policy $\rho$ & soft: \code{any\_of} preference & ignored (kernel class) \\
\code{cflags==-O2} & bit per flag & hard, overridden by local flag & ignored (kernel class) \\
\code{++foo} & bit per variant & hard, guarded per node & non-contradiction (gap) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The first two rows are lawful annotations: hard-or-soft, their node-local
truth adds nothing beyond what the plain form asserts, so ignoring them in
$\sat$ is sound and Theorem~\ref{thm:annotation} applies.  The third row is
the breach: \code{++foo} \emph{does} constrain the solution set (every node
defining \code{foo}), so ignoring it is not an option, and the node-local
approximation currently stored is neither sound nor complete for it ---
hence Counterexample~\ref{cex:prop} and the exclusion from $\Spec_0$.  The
meet conventions differ accordingly (promotion for the annotations,
demotion for the variant bit), and the variant row is slated to move out of
the annotation column altogether once the guard is stored
(\S\ref{sec:propagation}).

\section{Audit: the implementation against the framework}\label{sec:audit}

The framework was extracted from the implementation after the fact, so the
converse question is due: where does the implementation contradict it?  This
section reports an adversarial pass over the Python side, conducted with the
framework as the checklist --- every property named below (congruence of the
equalities, soundness and completeness of $\sat$ against $\gamma$,
statelessness, exactness of the meet) is one the preceding sections rely on.
Findings are grouped by failure type; every witness was executed on the
branch tip.  Some entries are new bugs, some are deliberate decisions whose
cost the framework makes visible; each is labeled.

\subsection{Equalities coarser than the order}\label{sec:audit-eq}

The framework wants state equality to refine $\ker$, which in turn refines
mutual satisfaction; three omissions in the comparison tuples break this.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Concreteness is not compared.}  \code{\_cmp\_node} omits
\code{\_concrete}, while \code{\_\_hash\_\_} and \code{satisfies} both branch
on it.\src{spec.py:4272--4284 vs 5421--5442}  Witness: mark one of two equal
dep-less specs concrete; then $a = b$ under \code{==} while
$\mathrm{hash}(a) \neq \mathrm{hash}(b)$ --- the \code{eq}/\code{hash}
contract is broken, so \code{Spec} is unsound as a dictionary key across the
abstract/concrete boundary --- and $a \not\sat b$ while $b \sat a$: equality
does not imply mutual satisfaction.  The state is reachable:
\code{from\_dict} marks deserialized roots concrete.  [new bug]
\item \emph{The variant type is not compared.}  \code{VariantValue.\_cmp\_iter}
omits \code{type}, which \code{\_\_str\_\_} and \code{yaml\_entry} both
read.\src{variant.py:406--411 vs 369--378, 495--512}  Two \code{==}-equal
variant values serialize as \code{foo:=bar} versus \code{foo=bar} and as
\code{['bar']} versus \code{'bar'} in the node dictionary --- equal states
with different documents, hence different hashes once concrete.  The state is
produced by \code{substitute\_abstract\_variants} and by the merge's type
narrowing.  [new bug]
\item \emph{A stale abstract hash survives concretization.}
\code{\_mark\_concrete} keeps \code{abstract\_hash}, which \code{\_cmp\_node}
still yields while satisfaction reads \code{dag\_hash()}: a concrete spec
built from \code{pkg-a/deadbeef} does not satisfy \code{pkg-a/deadbeef}.
[new bug, minor]
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{States denoting the empty set}\label{sec:audit-empty}

\S\ref{sec:empty} treated jointly-forced provider groups as the route to
$\den{s} = \emptyset$; the audit found a second route.  Concrete
\code{patches} values compare by string equality while the abstract side uses
the checksum-prefix rule,\src{variant.py:417--451} so a concrete prefix state
such as \code{patches:=aa} denotes $\emptyset$: a full checksum satisfies
\code{patches=aa} but not \code{patches:=aa}, which only the literal
two-character value equals.  That contradicts the documented invariant behind
\code{meet} that no spec denotes the empty set,\src{spec.py:5630--5636} and
the state is reachable only from input that writes it: variants print
exactly, and a concrete \emph{spec} renders its checksums as seven-character
\code{patches=} prefixes, a weaker constraint it satisfies.  Applying the
prefix rule in the concrete/concrete branch would close the route.  [new
bug]

\subsection{Statefulness at construction and comparison}\label{sec:audit-state}

The operations are repo-free (\S\ref{sec:repo}), but three construction-time
behaviors reintroduce environment dependence, and one comparison is partial.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Reserved OS names resolve against the host.}  The \code{os}
setter maps \code{default\_os}, \code{frontend}, \dots\ to the host
platform's values and fills \code{platform} in as a side
effect.\src{spec.py:539--562}  The same string denotes different sets on
different machines, and parsing is order-dependent:
\code{platform=test os=default\_os} and \code{os=default\_os platform=test}
fail with two different errors, the second a spurious duplicate-platform
report caused by the setter's fill-in.  [stateful; order-dependence new]
\item \emph{The variant point/lower-bound distinction is repo-decided.}
Whether \code{foobar=bar} is a point (\textsc{single}, concrete) or a lower
bound (\textsc{multi}, abstract) is decided by
\code{substitute\_abstract\_variants} from the package definition
(\S\ref{sec:repo}).  Before substitution
$\code{foobar=bar} \meet \code{foobar=baz}$ is defined; after it, undefined:
the projection $\pi_R$ moves states \emph{down} the preorder and shrinks the
domain of the meet.  So $\pi_R$ is a genuine strict refinement, not a
relabelling --- and a pre-substitution meet can be a state that only fails
later, as a different exception.  [framework-relevant; sharpens
\S\ref{sec:repo}]
\item \emph{Bare git refs make the operations partial, asymmetrically.}
\code{@git.main} against a version constraint resolves the ref through the
package's repository and network state; \code{satisfies} raises in one
operand order and returns \code{False} in the other, and \code{intersects}
raises in both.\src{version\_types.py:589--637, 878--896}  This is why
$\Spec_0$ excludes bare refs; the asymmetry itself was not previously
recorded.  [stateful, partial]
\item \emph{Frozen virtuals on an abstract node desynchronize the
operations.}  \code{\_satisfies\_node} reads \code{\_provided\_virtuals}
while the disjointness scan refuses the lookup by design; an abstract node
carrying a frozen map (preventable today only by convention) satisfies
\code{mpi@2} while not intersecting it --- $a \sat b$ with
$\den{a} \cap \den{b}$ reported empty.  [latent law violation]
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The target dimension}\label{sec:audit-target}

Two confirmed findings sharpen \S\ref{sec:arch}.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{\code{target=*} is classified concrete.}
\code{target\_concrete} tests only for \code{:} and \code{,}, so the wildcard
passes.\src{spec.py:728--733}  Downstream, the solver setup looks the
``concrete'' target up in the microarchitecture table and errors out:
\code{spack solve zlib target=*} fails outright while \code{target=x86\_64:}
and \code{os=*} solve.  The parser rejects \code{arch=*-*-*} plus a second
arch fragment as a duplicate, while the meet combines the same two
constraints happily --- and the meet \emph{manufactures} the state:
$\code{pkg-a} \meet \code{pkg-a target=*} = \code{pkg-a target=*}$, a meet of
two solvable specs that does not solve.  [new bug, end-to-end]
\item \emph{The per-interval cover is confirmed incomplete, and the meet is
not.}  Over the full x86\_64 microarchitecture table,
\code{target=nocona:haswell} and
\code{target=nocona:nehalem,}\allowbreak\code{nehalem:haswell}
denote the same seven targets, yet neither satisfies the other; two
different tilings of one interval are denotationally equal and mutually
unsatisfying, so satisfaction cannot recognize the canonical form of its own
meet: $m = L \meet R$ satisfies $L$ but $L \not\sat m$.  On the same data the
intersection itself was exact in every probed case --- the incompleteness is
confined to the containment test, and \code{\_canonical\_target\_range} never
fuses adjacent intervals (\code{X:Y,Y:Z} $\neq$ \code{X:Z} as states).
Completing the cover (containment in the union) and fusing adjacent
intervals in the canonical form would restore antisymmetry-as-state for this
dimension.  [new bug; resolves the former open question]
\end{enumerate}

Related completeness slack, same shape as the cover: $\gamma(\code{target=*})
= \gamma(\text{unset})$ --- every concrete spec has a target --- yet
\code{satisfies} places \code{*} strictly below unset; over an eight-atom
model of full arch triples, twenty such pairs are $\gamma$-comparable but
unsatisfied, while the meet was exact on all of them.  The wildcard is
denotationally the top of its dimension for \emph{node} attributes (an
architecture is always present), unlike on edges, where \code{\%c=*} differs
from no constraint because an edge can be absent.  [completeness slack]

\subsection{Annotations: three dimensions, three conventions}

\S\ref{sec:solver} required an annotation to be invisible to the order and
preserved by the meet.  Only compiler flags obey both halves.  Edges promote
(\textsc{none} $\meet$ \textsc{preference} $=$ \textsc{preference}); flags
promote; variants \emph{demote}: $\code{++foo} \meet \code{+foo} =
\code{+foo}$, discarding solver input exactly as bug no.~36 did for
edges.\src{variant.py:478--480 vs spec.py:1010--1013, 1165--1170}  Moreover
the variant bit is visible to \code{satisfies} with the inverted orientation
($\code{+foo} \sat \code{++foo}$ but not conversely), which is the
transitivity engine of Counterexample~\ref{cex:prop}.  Until the guard is
stored (\S\ref{sec:propagation}), no single annotation rule can be stated
for the algebra; the flag convention is the one the other two should
converge to.  [known gap, made line-precise]

\subsection{The dependency graph}\label{sec:audit-graph}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Provider identity is decided by child name, and a virtual is its
own rival.}  The one-provider-per-virtual scans compare edges by
\code{edge.spec.name}, so a placeholder child named after the virtual counts
as a second provider of itself.\src{spec.py:3619, 3660}  Three verified
consequences share this root cause.  (a)~\code{pkg-a \%[virtuals=mpi] mpi}
and \code{pkg-a \%[virtuals=mpi] mpich} do not intersect and their meet is
undefined, although \code{pkg-a \%mpi=mpich} satisfies both --- the meet's
totality axiom (M3) is violated.  (b)~The refusal runs before the absorption
scan, so the two orders of one spec string diverge:
\code{\%mpi=mpich \%mpi=mpi} parses to one absorbed edge, the reverse order
raises.  (c)~Worst, the same rule reaches
\code{\_condition\_can\_hold}, and canonicalization \emph{silently deletes}
a conditional edge whose condition the node in fact satisfies: a
\code{when} requiring the \code{mpi} provider is judged impossible on a node
that has one, and the dependent edge is dropped --- the parsed spec denotes
strictly more than its string, with no error raised.  [new bugs: totality,
order-invariance, unsound canonicalization]
\item \emph{Absorption re-checks the node with the wrong predicate.}  The
redundancy test's recursive fallback calls the full \code{satisfies}, whose
node comparison requires frozen provided virtuals; on abstract specs it
always fails, so a provider edge absorbs the virtual edge it satisfies only
when the virtual child has no dependencies.  State-level absorption fails on
exactly the graphs \code{from\_dict} and the solver produce.\src{spec.py:%
1893}  [new bug]
\item \emph{A ghost edge only \code{satisfies} can see.}  For pre-1.0
serializations, satisfaction synthesizes a direct \code{\%gcc} edge from
\code{annotations.compiler\_node\_attribute}, with a hardcoded
\code{virtuals=(c,cxx,fortran)} tuple.\src{spec.py:1734--1746}  The edge is
invisible to the edge maps, queries, \code{constrain}, \code{str} and
\code{to\_dict}; its virtuals are asserted, not read (any old spec satisfies
\code{\%[virtuals=fortran] gcc}); the annotation survives de-concretization,
where the meet then \emph{adds} a real node to a spec that already satisfied
the constraint --- the meet is not a lower bound as state.  Because no
equality reads annotations, two \code{==}-equal, \code{dag\_hash}-equal
specs have different meets, and \code{copy()} aliases the annotations
object, so mutating a copy flips the \emph{original's} \code{satisfies}
verdict.  [new bugs: congruence of all three equalities, aliasing]
\item \emph{\code{depflag = 0} has four readings, and one of them makes the
transitive search dead code.}  An unspecified deptype passes every deptype
filter in the edge queries, matches no deptype in the traversal seed,
is a subset lower bound in edge satisfaction, and selects nothing as a query
argument.\src{spec.py:1337, 1760, 1843, 1321}  Since the parser leaves every
abstract edge at \code{depflag = 0}, the depth-$\geq 2$ BFS of
Definition~\ref{def:simulation} never runs for parser-produced specs ---
\code{pkg-a \^{}pkg-b \%pkg-c} does not satisfy \code{pkg-a \^{}pkg-c}, and
reaches depth two only when every edge on the path carries explicit
link/run deptypes.  This refutes the stated justification of the
\code{nested\_direct\_edge} gap (``satisfies reaches it anyway: its BFS does
not stop at the first level''): the excuse holds for no spec the parser can
build, so the gap predicate is a mask, not a documented trade-off.
[decision needed; gap justification false]
\item \emph{Rename bookkeeping crashes on public paths.}  Two routes leave a
node filed in a parent's edge map under a stale key, and
\code{\_detach\_edge} then raises \code{KeyError}: constraining a node
obtained \emph{from an edge} (the anonymous child of
\code{pkg-a \^{}*+bvv} renamed in place by its own \code{constrain}), and
the concrete-rhs \code{\_dup} replacement, which restores dependents without
re-keying the parents' buckets.\src{spec.py:2424; 3455--3462}  The invariant
has a law (\code{edge\_maps\_keyed\_by\_name}), but the harness only
inspects top-level meet results of abstract operands, and neither route is
reachable that way.  [new bugs, crashes]
\item \emph{Construction can bypass canonicalization.}
\code{add\_dependency\_edge} neither canonicalizes conditions nor runs the
joint-emptiness scan, so it assembles a state that denotes $\emptyset$,
satisfies itself, and intersects nothing --- including the anonymous top ---
where the parser refuses the same spec.  Separately, the two-provider
refusal gates on object identity (\code{when is EMPTY\_SPEC}), so a
value-empty condition that is not the singleton bypasses
it.\src{spec.py:2256, 2368--2384}  [new bugs; the constructibility route
promised in \S\ref{sec:empty}]
\item \emph{Dead code with a backwards rule.}  \code{\_rename\_node} and
\code{\_merged\_when} are unreachable in any nontrivial form --- every
caller pairs edges that already share a child name --- and
\code{\_merged\_when}'s fallback computes the \emph{meet} of two conditions
where the contravariant reading requires their join, contradicting its own
docstring.  Harmless today; a landmine for whoever relaxes the
same-dependency test.\src{spec.py:852--861, 1277--1293}  [latent]
\item \emph{Joint emptiness is visible to \code{intersects} and invisible
to \code{satisfies}.}  The disjointness scan runs a running merge over a
whole same-name group of direct edges, while the satisfaction-side condition
checks are pairwise; a group of conditional edges that jointly falsify their
own condition is seen by one and not the other, so \code{satisfies} demands
a witness for a condition that can never hold.  The affected specs denote
nonempty sets and are inside $\Spec_0$: this is completeness slack of
$\sat$, not a preorder failure.\src{spec.py:3622--3639 vs 1788,
3642--3671}  [completeness]
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-operation coherence}\label{sec:audit-crossop}

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{A spec a user can type fails \code{self\_meet}.}  The parser
appends flags without deduplication, so \code{pkg-a cflags=-O2 cflags==-O2}
holds the same string twice with different propagation bits; constraining
the spec \emph{with itself} returns \code{True} and rewrites the state
(both copies promoted), and repeated merges duplicate values into states
such as \code{cflags=='-O2 -O2'} that re-parse differently.  No gap
predicate excuses this family.  [new bug]
\item \emph{Serialization deduplicates by hashes that mutation does not
invalidate.}  \code{to\_dict} identifies nodes by their cached DAG hash;
\code{constrain} never clears caches, and the solver force-caches hashes on
abstract specs.  After forcing and constraining, a spec serializes under its
stale identity, and two distinct children collapse into one node on the way
back --- a constraint applied to one edge leaks onto the other, and the
round-trip is no longer a bijection.\src{spec.py:2941--2946; solver/asp.py:%
328}  [new bug]
\item \emph{The three equalities are pairwise incomparable.}
\code{to\_dict} also identifies structurally equal sibling nodes, so
\code{from\_dict} returns a graph with \emph{shared} children;
\code{\_cmp\_iter}'s deep case observes sharing (ids by object identity)
while its shallow case cannot, so the round-trip is \code{to\_dict}-equal
and \code{==}-different for one spec shape and \code{==}-equal for another.
Together with the annotations finding above: none of \code{==},
\code{to\_dict}, \code{dag\_hash} refines another.  [new bug]
\item \emph{External attributes exist only for serialization.}
\code{external\_path} is written by \code{to\_dict}, read by no equality, no
disjointness check, and no merge: the meet keeps the left operand's value
and silently drops the right's, so the meet is not commutative as state and
\code{constrain} discards external data.\src{spec.py:2801--2806,
3673--3727}  [new bug]
\item \emph{\code{deps=False} is incoherent against a concrete operand.}
The disjointness scan ignores the \code{deps} argument whenever either side
is concrete, while \code{satisfies} honors it: a concrete spec satisfies
\code{libdwarf \^{}pkg-b} with \code{deps=False} while \code{intersects} is
false and \code{constrain} raises.  All current \code{deps=False} callers
pass abstract operands, so the violation is latent.\src{spec.py:3485--3491
vs 3859--3861}  [latent law violation]
\item \emph{Validation cannot represent anonymous nodes.}
\code{validate\_or\_raise} on any spec with an anonymous node crashes with
\code{IndexError} (the name guard checks virtualness but not emptiness), an
exception outside the \code{SpecError} hierarchy every caller guards
for.\src{spec.py:3411}  Similarly, comparing bare git refs raises
\code{VersionLookupError}, which is also not a \code{SpecError}, so the
partiality of \S\ref{sec:audit-state} escapes the algebra's own exception
guards.  [new bug; hazard]
\item \emph{Concrete providers break the atoms model.}  A concrete
\code{gcc} with frozen virtuals satisfies the bare virtual \code{c}, yet
\code{Spec("c")} and \code{Spec("gcc")} do not intersect and their meet is
undefined --- an atom below both operands of an empty meet, the backward
direction of the exact-GLB law.  The name-disjointness-by-fiat rule of
\S\ref{sec:names} and the frozen-virtual satisfaction rule of
\S\ref{sec:repo} are individually principled and jointly incomplete; the
harness cannot see it because its atom universe contains no concrete specs.
[completeness]
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Smaller findings}

The namespace dimension is lawful in isolation (flat, top, disjoint named
namespaces), but an \emph{anonymous} node may carry a namespace, and the
merge stamps it onto whatever named node it meets --- asserting a repo
membership the operand never claimed, and rewriting \code{pkg-b} to
\code{builtin\_mock.pkg-b} with no denotational narrowing (in a
configuration, one repo provides each package).  This is the precise content
of the harness's namespace gap.  [law strain]  \code{Spec.\_dup} computes its
\code{changed} flag with conjunction instead of disjunction; every call site
discards the value, so the defect is dead until a caller trusts
it.\src{spec.py:4031--4044}  [latent]  \code{Spec.\_\_hash\_\_} omits
compiler flags for dep-less abstract specs --- hash collisions only, the
contract holds.  [fine]

\subsection{What the audit says about the framework, and about the harness}

Every confirmed finding lands in a category the framework names: a
comparison tuple omitting a dimension some operation reads (congruence), a
wildcard mistaken for a point (soundness), a cover test weaker than the
denotation (completeness), a construction consulting ambient state
(statelessness), a merge discarding solver input (annotation preservation).
None of them required new theory to state --- which is the practical case
for the framework: it converts ``various ad-hoc decisions'' from folklore
into checkable propositions, each with a one-line classification and, where
broken, a witness.

The audit also maps the law harness's blind spots, since every finding above
survives a clean harness run.  The generators draw no concrete operands, so
every concrete-side fast path is unchecked; the laws compare states only
with \code{to\_dict()}, so \code{==} and \code{dag\_hash} incoherences are
invisible; \code{deps=False}, \code{\_\_contains\_\_},
\code{validate\_or\_raise} and \code{add\_dependency\_edge} are outside the
tested surface; each dimension contributes one fragment per operand, so
duplicate-flag states are never drawn; and specs are only ever produced by
parse and meet, so states reachable through \code{from\_dict}, forced hash
caches, or in-place mutation of a node borrowed from an edge are missed.
Finally, gap predicates deserve the same skepticism as laws: one of them
(\code{nested\_direct\_edge}) excuses failures with a justification the
audit refuted.  Each of these is a concrete, bounded extension of the
harness.

The division of labor is deliberate.  The Lean side proves the
\emph{metatheory}: \emph{any} operation satisfying (M1)--(M3) over
\emph{any} preorder yields the quotient semilattice with equational laws,
annotations invisible to any order collapse in its quotient, products and
simulations preserve preordering.  These are the statements for which testing
is the wrong tool (they quantify over all inputs and all implementations)
and proof is cheap.  The Python side is checked against \emph{instances} of
the axioms --- (M1)--(M3), reflexivity, transitivity, congruence --- by
exhaustive corpus sweeps and randomized property search.  The two sides meet
at the axioms: the laws in \code{spec\_algebra\_properties.py} are, nearly
clause for clause, the hypotheses of the Lean theorems.

\subsection{Can proof reach the implementation?}\label{sec:bridge}

Abstractly: the Lean development and \code{spec.py} live in different worlds,
and no amount of theory-side proving makes Python code correct by itself.
The gap can be narrowed in well-understood stages, each buying a specific
guarantee:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Shared axioms (the current state).}  The specification is proved
coherent: the laws are mutually consistent, and they entail the intended
structure (Theorems~\ref{thm:meetlaws}, \ref{thm:descent} show the tested
laws are not an arbitrary checklist but exactly a partial-meet presentation
of a semilattice --- and conversely, that testing (M1)--(M3) suffices; the
equational laws come for free).  The implementation is tested against the
axioms.  The residual risk is coverage: a law can hold on every generated
input and fail on an ungenerated one.  The generator's known blind spots are
themselves documented as gap predicates, which is unusual hygiene, but
blind spots they remain.
\item \emph{Executable reference model.}  The Lean definitions are already
constructive; making the mini-model's order and meet decidable and compiling
it (Lean~4 compiles to native code) yields an oracle that \emph{provably}
satisfies the laws.  Differential testing --- serialize a spec pair, ask
Python and the oracle the same question, compare verdicts --- upgrades
law-checking to equivalence-checking against a verified object: any
divergence is a bug in the Python (or in the encoding), even when the
Python's answer happens to satisfy every law on that input.  The encoding of
\code{Spec} into the model becomes the new trusted boundary, and the model
must grow the dimensions it abstracts (versions, targets, conditions) to be
a discriminating oracle.
\item \emph{Verified implementation.}  Proving \code{spec.py} itself is not
realistic (dynamic language, no practical semantics); the honest endpoint of
this road is extraction: a verified core (in Lean or another prover) that
\emph{is} the implementation of the order and meet, called from Python, with
the property harness retained to check the binding.  That is a large
engineering decision, not a formalization step.
\end{enumerate}

Stage 2 is the natural next increment for this codebase: the 864-atom
universe of \code{exact\_glb} is already a hand-rolled reference model; the
Lean file is its statement with proofs.  Merging the two --- one model,
proved lawful, compiled, and wired into the corpus runner as an oracle ---
would close the loop at modest cost.

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

\begin{openq}
\emph{Flags.}  What is the lawful algebra for order-significant flag
sequences?  Candidates: subsequence order with shuffle-merge meets (the meet
is then a set of minimal merges, not a single state), or an
interleaving-indifferent quotient with a canonical order.  The current
``sequence state, set order, append merge'' has no commutative meet even up
to $\ker$-excused pairs.
\end{openq}

\begin{openq}
\emph{Bottom.}  Should the algebra admit $\bot$ (\S\ref{sec:empty})?  With
$\bot$, \code{intersects} could be complete for the denotational reading
(``$\den{a} \cap \den{b} = \emptyset$ iff the meet is $\bot$''), and
reflexivity would be unconditional; without it, emptiness must be
unrepresentable, which neither the pairwise condition checks nor the
construction bypasses of \S\ref{sec:audit-graph} currently guarantee.
\end{openq}

\begin{openq}
\emph{$\pi_R$ as an adjoint.}  Does the construction-time projection have a
universal property --- $\pi_R(s)$ the $\sat$-greatest $R$-valid spec below
$s$ --- and do freezing and concretization commute with meets:
$\pi_R(a \meet b) \ker \pi_R(a) \meet \pi_R(b)$ where defined?  The audit
sharpened the stakes: $\pi_R$ strictly shrinks the domain of the meet
(\S\ref{sec:audit-state}), so commutation is the best one can hope for, not
a triviality.
\end{openq}

\begin{openq}
\emph{The normative \code{depflag = 0}.}  The audit found four readings of
an unspecified dependency type (\S\ref{sec:audit-graph}); which is
normative?  The subset/lower-bound reading of edge satisfaction suggests
$0$ should behave as the top of the deptype dimension (matched by
everything, traversed everywhere); the conservative traversal reading
suggests the opposite.  A decision here determines whether the transitive
reach of Definition~\ref{def:simulation} is real or vestigial for parsed
specs.
\end{openq}

\begin{openq}
\emph{One state identity.}  \code{==}, \code{to\_dict()} and
\code{dag\_hash} are pairwise incomparable on the current state space
(\S\ref{sec:audit-crossop}).  Which is the canonical identity the others
must refine?  The framework's answer would be: state equality is
\code{to\_dict()} equality, \code{==} refines it modulo the annotation
dimensions of \S\ref{sec:solver}, and both refine $\ker$ --- but that is a
target, not the status quo.
\end{openq}

\begin{remark}
Two earlier open questions were settled by the audit: target containment is
confirmed incomplete, with the fix identified
(\S\ref{sec:audit-target}); and the per-edge existential witness search was
probed for a global-matching counterexample and none exists at the abstract
level --- the same pairwise implication that lets one candidate edge witness
two constraint edges also fuses those constraint edges when they are built
into one spec, so search and meet are consistent by construction.
\end{remark}
